From Itahari to St. John's: Balancing an MSc, a Dev Job and Freelance Clients

What changed when I moved from Nepal to St. John's, Newfoundland to study an MSc in Computer Science at MUN while working as a software developer and keeping my freelance clients.

For years my working day started in Itahari, Nepal, a few hours ahead of my UK clients. Now it starts in St. John's, Newfoundland, where I'm studying for an MSc in Computer Science at Memorial University (MUN) and working as a Software Developer at Verisult. I still run freelance projects on PeoplePerHour.

This post covers how I try to make the three fit together. It's half reflection and half a set of notes I wish I'd had before the move.

The time zone flipped, and that helped

From Nepal (UTC+5:45), UK business hours ran from my mid-afternoon until late at night. Newfoundland Time is UTC−3:30, only 3.5 hours behind London, which is one of the best overlaps you can get in North America:

  • Morning in St. John's = early afternoon in the UK. I can answer client messages before classes.
  • Afternoon in St. John's = working hours in Toronto and Vancouver, so Canadian clients are easy too.
  • I no longer have to deploy at midnight to stay outside UK peak hours.

Three commitments, one calendar

The biggest change is that my time now belongs to three groups of people: professors, my team at Verisult and freelance clients. What has worked so far:

  1. Fixed blocks, not leftover time. Coursework, Verisult and freelance work each get named blocks in the calendar. Freelance work fits into its blocks, and I don't eat into the others.
  2. Fewer, larger freelance projects. One well-scoped retainer is far easier to manage than five small jobs.
  3. Written updates instead of calls. A short async update ("done / next / blocked") saves a meeting and gives the client a record.
  4. Saying no early. If a project would clash with exams, I say so at quote time, not mid-sprint.

What the MSc adds to client work

I went back to university to get deeper in the fundamentals behind the work I already do every day. Topics like algorithms, distributed systems and machine learning quickly turn into practical questions: why is this query slow, what happens to this queue under load, where would a model actually help this business. The research mindset also makes me better at measuring before optimising, a habit I write about in my Laravel performance post.

Notes for developers making a similar move

  • Update every profile on the same day. Location and time zone on your portfolio, LinkedIn, freelance profiles and email signature. Clients notice inconsistencies.
  • Keep your tools portable. Password manager, SSH keys, 2FA backup codes and server access should all work from a new laptop in a new country before you fly.
  • Tell existing clients early. Explain the move, the new hours and the overlap. Most of them will care only about response times.
  • Check your study and work permit conditions before taking on any additional work, and keep your records tidy.
